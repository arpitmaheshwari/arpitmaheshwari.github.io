# Narrative Frameworks for the Case Studies
*Research distillation, 2026-08-14. Reader: a design leader at an AI company reviewing
10–20 portfolios in a sitting. Goal: intriguing, memorable, forces the interview.*

## What the evidence says (sources verified this session)

**From the exemplars.** Simon Pan's Uber case — the one hiring managers keep citing —
opens cold on a business number ("over $1M wasted per week"), runs Challenge →
Insights → **Reframe** → Redesign → Launch → Impact, and its hinge is the reframe
beat: the moment the obvious problem turns out to be the wrong problem. Indeed
Design's guidance: two paragraphs of context maximum, "one user flow is enough to
prove you can make a user flow," and "a glorious journey will always be more
compelling than a clinical list of events."

**From hiring managers.** Tami Evnin (Glassdoor design lead): reviewers do 10–20
portfolios in one sitting — "help me find the signal amongst the noise," "lead with
visual artifacts and fill in the story with words," "don't make me wait too long to
get to the punch line," and self-awareness about failure "earns the interview."
Across manager write-ups the consistent asks: judgment over process theatre, the
candidate's exact role, impact without overclaiming, named collaboration.

**From the science.**
- *Narrative transportation* (Green & Brock): a reader immersed in a story
  counterargues less and the persuasion lasts (measured at two weeks+). Immersion
  needs concrete imagery and someone to care about — abstractions break it.
- *Peak–end rule* (Kahneman & Fredrickson): memory keeps the most intense moment
  and the ending, not the average. A uniformly thorough case is a forgettable one:
  engineer ONE peak, and land the ending deliberately.
- *Information-gap theory* (Loewenstein): curiosity is a drive state triggered by a
  SPECIFIC, salient gap close to what the reader already knows — and a small priming
  dose of information increases it. An unresolved concrete question pulls a reader
  through a page better than any amount of polish.

## Rules that apply regardless of framework (the floor)
1. **First 100 words earn the next 500.** Open on the tension, never on context.
2. **One peak per case.** Decide which moment is the peak before writing; build to it.
3. **The ending is engineered.** Boundary ("where this wouldn't transfer") + door
   (the reference, the demo, the next case). Never trail off into links.
4. **Artifacts carry argument.** Every caption states what the reader should conclude,
   not what the image is. A viewer who reads only captions gets the whole case.
5. **The failure beat is mandatory.** One genuine wrong turn, told plainly. (Evnin:
   this earns interviews; it is also true.)
6. **Two ledgers, never mixed.** Numbers I can defend vs numbers on the public
   record, attributed. Already house law; the frameworks inherit it.
7. **Facts are locked to CANONICAL-FACTS.md.** A framework changes order and voice,
   never claims.

## The three contenders (each rendered on the AdTech case in prototypes/)

### F1 · THE VERDICT — answer-first, evidence after
*Minto's pyramid × Evnin's "don't make me wait."*
Spine: **Verdict** (the outcome as a scene, first 3 sentences) → **The bet** (what I
believed that others didn't) → **Proof I was right** (each section heading is a
claim; its body is the evidence; artifacts as exhibits) → **What it cost** (the
failure beat + tradeoffs) → **The boundary** (where it wouldn't transfer) → door.
Reads complete from headings alone — built for the 90-second skim that decides
whether the 10-minute read happens. Peak: the mechanism reveal. Risk: least
suspense; feels like a brief, not a story.

### F2 · THE ANOMALY — a mystery the reader solves alongside me
*Loewenstein's gap × narrative transportation, with a skimmer's escape hatch.*
Spine: **Cold open on the anomaly** (a true, specific contradiction: "the model beat
the traders every week; adoption was zero") → **Docket card** (for skimmers: the
verdict + numbers up top, in a sealed-file visual so readers can choose suspense) →
**The suspects** (the obvious explanations, tried and eliminated — my own wrong
diagnosis is suspect #1) → **The evidence** (research artifacts as clues) → **The
turn** (the reframe — peak) → **The mechanism** → **The receipts** → case closed +
what I keep. Risk: the withheld punchline irritates a skimmer — mitigated by the
docket card. Strongest transportation; strongest memorability.

### F3 · THE ARGUMENT — every case proves one stealable principle
*The essay structure; positions the author as someone with a worldview.*
Spine: **Thesis** (one transferable sentence a design leader could quote in their
next exec review: "accuracy is table stakes; trust is a mechanism you design") →
**Stakes** (what believing the opposite cost this client) → **The test** (the
project as the thesis's trial, including the beat where the thesis nearly failed) →
**The mechanism** (how the principle became interface) → **Falsifiable evidence** →
**Where the principle breaks** (the boundary as intellectual honesty). Each of the
six cases proves a DIFFERENT principle → the portfolio becomes a system of ideas,
which is what separates a lead from a senior. Risk: drifts abstract if any claim
beat loses its concrete scene — rule: no paragraph without a person, a number, or
an object in it.
