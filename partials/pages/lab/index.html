The evidence boundary

These prove less than they look like they prove

Three different things get called evidence. Only the first lives on this page.

Implementation
The code does what it says. 76 assertions across 42 tests, run in your browser above, on the same module the demos import. That is all a green test proves.
Usage
The rules came out of shipped products, not a notebook. The verbs, the abstention, the logged override and the citation gate were each argued for and built inside the case studies. The code here is my restatement of those decisions, not the client’s source.
Outcome
Whether a pattern moves adoption is a claim about a product, not about a function. Where I have a measured before-and-after, it sits on the case with its baseline and window. A passing suite will never stand in for a result here.
Where the line sits. I ship the front-end of what I design, in the pull request under my name, with senior engineers reviewing. These artifacts are pure functions by choice, not by ceiling: logic any stack can adopt and any test can pin down. The stateful counterpart is the book edition of this site, about 150 KB of my own React. What I don’t claim is the backend. That is the honest boundary, and it is the same one I give on the technical screen.

Why a lab at all

A principle with no failing state is a preference

The eight patterns in the pattern library came out of shipped products, and for years they lived where principles usually live: in slides, in review comments, in my head. Reasonable people agreed with every one of them and shipped the naked confidence score anyway. A principle you can nod at costs nothing to ignore.

Written as code, the same rules get a way to fail. decide() reports a review with no reasons as a violation, not a suggestion. calibrate() returns insufficient-data instead of a flattering number when the sample is thin. trustlint refuses to score truth at all, and says so in its own output.

That is what the Lab is for: the judgments I keep making in design reviews, given somewhere to fail, so they hold when I am not in the room.

The thresholds here are defaults, not findings. Where a band starts is a product decision someone owns and revisits — the rule is only that a score lands on one verb, never that 0.7 is the right place to put the line.

The system I leave behind

The system I leave behind is one a machine can read

Six products at Talon had grown one at a time and looked like six companies had made them. For a company selling one integrated platform, that was the claim failing in plain sight. I introduced one design system across the line, so Plato, Ada and Atlas read as rooms of one building. Then I encoded it as a Claude plugin, so it stopped depending on who remembered it.

One family
Family resemblance, not a coat of paint. For a company selling an integrated platform, looking integrated is the claim, made visible.
Encoded, not documented
Two systems shipped as installable plugins inside client organisations. A Figma library is applied by whoever remembers it. A system a machine reads is applied every time.
Enforced
Contrast measured from rendered pixels before every push; overflow and legibility swept nightly. The interface I design is the front-end I ship in the pull request.
