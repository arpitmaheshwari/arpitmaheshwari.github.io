The contract I write when a capability needs a boundary

A language model has no edges. So I draw them.

Ask one anything and it answers — fluently, in or out of its depth. A user's trust does not erode slowly; it dies in a single confident wrong answer. So before launch I write a capability contract — a one-pager the whole team signs — and it goes in the interface, at the point of use.

In scope

It's for

The job stated narrowly — “score deal risk from the data room,” not “analyse documents.”

Declines

It can't

A named limit, not a hedge. “I can't price illiquid assets” — never “results may vary.”

Hands back

Ask a human

A visible route to a person, designed with the care of the happy path. Never a silent, confident guess.

How I lead — come argue with me

Week one: I'm reading your evals and sitting silent on customer calls. By week two we're arguing productively.

With engineering

Eval design before interface design

The front-end I own ships in the PR — commented and ready for review. When we disagree on feasibility, the cheapest experiment goes first and settles it.

With product / founder

Outcomes, not deliverables

I'll contest the roadmap when the numbers contradict it. The design doc is mine to write; the spec is yours.

With customers

Five calls in week one

One a week forever after, and I read the raw support tickets myself. No AI feature ships until I've personally watched someone fail to use it.
