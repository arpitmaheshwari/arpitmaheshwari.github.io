## The award-portfolio formula (subitem pattern across Freelance/Agency Portfolios)
1. Preloader (3s of a 15s attention budget — collection of 1562 says everyone has one)
2. Giant name hero, often kinetic/marquee type
3. Hover-distortion or WebGL project list (thumbnails, no outcomes)
4. "Selected works 2019–2026" with client logos
5. Oversized email in footer, magnetic buttons, custom cursor
6. Dark mode default, electric accent, Druk/Aeonik/Editorial New/GT flavor fonts
→ Formula optimizes for other designers (awards juries), NOT for hiring managers scanning 40 tabs. Decoration-forward, proof-absent.

## What's rare in the canon (= opportunity)
- Content-first structure: Storytelling + Microcopy collections are followed but thin — copy design is the least-imitated craft.
- Outcomes/numbers on the surface: project pages bury results below media.
- Decision-level evidence (why, tradeoffs, before/after) — essentially absent from the canon.
- Scannability: almost no award site passes "read only the largest text = full pitch".
- Fast first paint with zero preloader is itself differentiating.

## Design implications for this brief
- No preloader, no custom cursor, no marquee, no hover-distortion galleries, no card grids.
- Largest-text-only reading path must carry: name → what → proof number → why keep reading.
- Structure/scale/rhythm carry originality; interaction only where it IS the evidence (e.g. a live artifact).
- Two typefaces max at extreme scale contrast; quiet single-column body; ≥11px floor (WCAG AA contrast, prefers-reduced-motion respected).
- One signature moment that demonstrates skill, not claims it; four exit paths (skim, forward, call, artifact) as first-class design objects.
