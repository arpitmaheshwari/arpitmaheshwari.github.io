Option 1 Editorial clarity Source Serif 4 · Source Sans 3 · JetBrains Mono

A true superfamily: serif and sans drawn together by the same team to share proportions, so headline and body feel like one voice instead of two fonts introduced to each other. Source Sans 3 has a tall x-height and open apertures — it holds up at 11px labels where your monospace currently struggles. Optical sizing means the display face thins gracefully at 44px and thickens at 17px.

Strongest for SCANNING · superfamily coherence · optical size axis · 8 weights, all open source

Option 2 Maximum accessibility Literata · Atkinson Hyperlegible · JetBrains Mono

Atkinson Hyperlegible was drawn by the Braille Institute specifically so low-vision readers can tell characters apart — capital I, lowercase l and digit 1 are all distinct; O and 0 cannot be confused. Literata was commissioned by Google for long-form reading on screens. The argument beyond legibility: your site's thesis is designing for people who aren't you, and this is that thesis in the typeface itself — a thing you can say in an interview.

Strongest for ACCESSIBILITY · disambiguated letterforms · built for low vision · reads warmer, slightly quirkier

Option 3 Modern neutral Newsreader · Inter · JetBrains Mono

Keeps the display serif you already have — the face doing most of your brand work — and replaces only the body and label roles. Inter is the most-tested interface typeface on the web: tall x-height, huge weight range, engineered for screens at small sizes. The least disruptive of the four, and the safest on unusual devices. The cost: Inter is everywhere, so it reads as competent rather than distinctive.

Lowest RISK · keeps existing display identity · ubiquitous rendering track record

Option 4 Civic plain Public Sans (display + body) · DM Mono

No serif at all. Public Sans is the US government's design-system face, drawn for public information that everyone must be able to read — the headline and body are one family at different weights, which is the most scannable arrangement possible. This is the biggest departure: it retires the editorial serif entirely and makes the site read as an instrument rather than an essay.

Most SCANNABLE · single-family hierarchy · government accessibility standard · loses the editorial voice
