Receipt — campaign planning, 2 wks → 3 hrs

Claim
Planning fell from two weeks to three hours, with an average media-value gain of £69,000 per client.
Context
The UK’s largest out-of-home advertising company — £400M annual revenue, 40% of the UK market. The model beat the traders — and they overrode its recommendations anyway, until each one arrived as a full campaign plan with KPIs attached and one-click customisation.
My role
Owned product definition, roadmap, and end-to-end design across all six systems of the aggregator — campaign planner, audience intelligence, creative management, play reporting, the free inventory SaaS for media owners, and a white-label booking product franchisors give their franchisees — unified under one design system I introduced — later packaged as a Claude plugin so developers could build on-system without me.
How measured
Per campaign, against the ~2-week manual planning baseline. The three hours belong to the new workflow, not to raw model speed: the engine was already fast and already winning on the scoreboard. What changed is that traders acted on its output instead of redoing the work by hand — which is the part I designed.
Not mine
The bidding model and the 50M-bids/hour infrastructure — engineering’s win, and they own it.
Named & public
The client is named — Talon Outdoor — and its leaders are on the record: UK bookings through the platform went from under 5% to 100% in about a year (their CPO). The 3-hour figure itself is measured inside the engagement, and mine to defend.

Receipt — deal screening, 60% faster

Claim
Private-equity deal screening ran 60% faster once analysts stopped re-verifying the machine.
Context
An LLM over deal documents. The model was already good enough for the workflow; adoption was the bottleneck — analysts are paid to doubt a confident number, so every claim had to trace to its source, with honest abstention when sources were thin.
My role
Product definition, the interaction design, and the abstention + citation UX — built before the first release, so every claim traced to its source on day one.
How measured
Pre- vs post-rollout. Sources were exposed beside every score; the common case showed three.
Not mine
The model’s accuracy — the ML team’s result to defend — and the retrieval backend.
Limitation
NDA: the sample, eval design, and baseline are walked through on a call — artifacts open.

03 The method — the contract, then the workflow

A language model has no edges. So I draw them.

Ask one anything and it answers — fluently, in or out of its depth. A user’s trust does not erode slowly; it dies in a single confident wrong answer. So before launch I write a capability contract — a one-pager the whole team signs — and it goes in the interface, at the point of use.

In scope

It’s for

The job stated narrowly — “score deal risk from the data room,” not “analyse documents.”

Declines

It can’t

A named limit, not a hedge. “I can’t price illiquid assets” — never “results may vary.”

Hands back

Ask a human

A visible route to a person, designed with the care of the happy path. Never a silent, confident guess.
